## Research programme

**Full research programme and complete 58-record catalogue:** [English](RESEARCH_PROGRAM.md) · [中文](RESEARCH_PROGRAM.zh.md)


I develop theoretical and methodological work on factor hierarchies, structural-regime switching, statistical representations, information geometry, and scientific testability. The research programme includes the **Factor Hierarchy System**, **Switch Measure Theory**, the **Information Isomorphism Law**, the **Testability Principle**, and the **Testability Norms**.

## Four proposed components

| Layer | Component | Function in the proposed framework |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Epistemological foundation | **Information Isomorphism Law** | Studies when statistical structure carries information about regime structure. |
| Ontology | **Factor Hierarchy Law** | Distinguishes regime factors from execution factors and studies their interactions and switching roles. |
| Epistemological standard | **Testability Principle** | Proposes operational tests for claims about factor importance in multi-factor systems. |
| Methodology | **Testability Norms** | Organizes testing into six functional dimensions while treating implementation tools as replaceable. |

The same programme is presented under three names for different purposes: **Factor Hierarchy System** (natural-science framing), **Information Isomorphism Epistemology** (philosophical framing), and **Scientific Discovery Operating System** (methodological/engineering framing). These are the author's conceptual framings, not institutional affiliations.

## Structural–Regime foundations and unified metatheory

A connected reading path runs from **2026ay to 2026bf**. The paper **2026bf explicitly synthesizes the seven preceding works, 2026ay–2026be**. For navigation, I describe these eight works as **“Structural–Regime Foundations, Interfaces, and Unified Metatheory.”** This is an editorial grouping based on shared mathematical objects and stated dependencies, not a claim that every paper was originally deposited under a formally named series.

1. **2026ay — Geometry and identifiability:** [Relative-norm observations, conformal quotients, and Fisher–Finsler boundaries](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23226534)
2. **2026az — Information and mechanism identifiability:** [Multiscale mechanistic roles in finite-observation systems](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23235287)
3. **2026ba — Dynamics and statistical transitions:** [Structural stability, bifurcation, observation, and critical scaling](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23252528)
4. **2026bb — Hopf observability:** [Mode visibility and statistical transfer](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23252668)
5. **2026bc — Local information geometry:** [Controlled probes, threshold relative entropy, and nonregular resolution](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23252900)
6. **2026bd — Stochastic statistical visibility:** [Bigraded filtrations, successive obstructions, and Fisher–KL transfer](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23253238)
7. **2026be — Relative module basis:** [Constructive irreducibility and the dynamical–Fisher–statistical interface](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23255877)
8. **2026bf — Unified metatheory:** [Transformation certificates, semantic obstructions, module–obstruction duality, and computability boundaries](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23256372)

Two closely related programme-level works precede this eight-paper arc: [2026aw, the axiomatic foundations of Switch Measure Theory](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22806571), and [2026ax, the problem-generation framework](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22809598). I do **not** label the group a “First-Principles Series”: “foundations and interfaces” better reflects the heterogeneous contributions and avoids implying that all results are deduced from one universal axiom system.

## Research areas represented in the catalogue

- **Finance and asset pricing:** dual-anchor pricing, yield-spread regimes, government-bond valuation, exchange rates, and capital flows.
- **Astronomy:** regime-dependent measurement and stellar-model consistency, including the proposed “Tang Break” around the K/G transition region.
- **Statistical physics:** structural-break analyses of 2D and 3D Ising models.
- **Particle physics:** regime-boundary analyses using particle-data patterns.
- **Chemistry:** regime-based analyses of periodic trends and the proposed calcium–lanthanum singularity results.
- **Nuclear physics:** regime-switching analyses of shell structure, magic numbers, and nuclide maps.
